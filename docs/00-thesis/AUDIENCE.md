# Audience

Everyone applying for work and struggling. One product, with no separate versions by age.

| Group | Main struggle | Message | Where to reach them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gen Z | First job, entry-level posts asking for experience | See which employers still answer | TikTok, Reels, Shorts |
| Millennials | Layoffs, career changes, bills, little time | See who replies before you spend the evening | Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn posts |
| Gen X and older | Longer searches, age bias | Plain facts on where applying is worth your time | YouTube, Facebook |

These channels are judgment, not measured.

## Rules

- Group data by career stage, field and location. **Never collect or show age.** It adds legal risk and invites unfair comparisons.
- Plain words and readable type. No slang aimed at one generation.
- Reply rates by career stage come once there is enough data, because a company can answer seniors and ignore new graduates.

## Evidence, from search summaries (open before quoting)

- Unemployment averaged 19 weeks for ages 25 to 34 and 26 weeks for ages 55 to 64 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/baby-boomers-gen-xers-laid-off-still-unemployed-take-pay-cuts-to-work/)).
- Nearly half of Gen Z and millennials feel subscribed to too many services, against 29% of boomers ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/research/survey-overpaying-for-subscriptions)).
- A third of recruiters in a GMAC survey say they are replacing entry-level roles with AI ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/26/gen-z-entry-level-jobs-replaced-by-ai-new-gmac-recruiters-survey-tech-manufacturing-jobs-most-at-risk/)).
- 79% of hiring managers in a Harris Poll see more older workers applying for entry-level jobs ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/work/careers/older-workers-entry-level-jobs/)).
